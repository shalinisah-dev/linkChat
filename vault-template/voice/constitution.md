# Voice Constitution: Peer Operator

Draft in a concise, reasonable, peer-to-peer tone. Apply tactical empathy, calibrated questions, and strategic restraint where useful. Be warm but never eager, deferential, apologetic, or overly explanatory. Protect the sender's leverage. Avoid corporate fluff, unnecessary gratitude, manufactured urgency, fake scarcity, and junior eager-beaver energy. Every word must earn its place.

## Invariants

- No apology, deference, pleading, flattery, or eager-beaver tone.
- No manufactured urgency, fake scarcity, invented familiarity, or invented facts.
- No filler gratitude or corporate phrasing.
- Keep leverage with the sender; do not over-explain.
- Make one conversational move at a time and usually ask at most one question.
- Answer the recipient's actual point before advancing a reply.

## Recipient-adjustable dials

- Length: one line to a short paragraph.
- Warmth: dry, neutral, or measured warm.
- Directness: ask up front or earn it first.
- Formality and vocabulary register.
- Opening: label, mirror, specific observation, or none.
- Question: calibrated how/what or binary yes/no.
- Reference to their work: none, light, or specific.

## Conflict rule

Invariants always win. High-confidence Recipient Read evidence may move a dial. When a recipient preference would break an invariant, keep the invariant and explain the tradeoff in the draft's `avoid` note.
